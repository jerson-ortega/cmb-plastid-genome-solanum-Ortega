

**1. What is the scientific name of your chosen plant and its plastid genome accession number?**
* Scientific Name: Solanum lycopersicum
* Accession Number: NC_007898.3

**2. What is the total length of the plastid genome in base pairs?**
The total length is 155,461 bp.

**3. Can you identify the LSC, SSC, IRA, and IRB regions on your map? Briefly describe their positions.**
Yes. The Large Single-Copy (LSC) region forms the largest continuous section of the outer circle. Opposite to it is the Small Single-Copy (SSC) region. Separating these two single-copy regions on both sides are the two Inverted Repeat regions (IRA and IRB), which are represented by distinct segments on the map indicating duplicated regions.

**4. Give three examples of genes located in the LSC region.**
1. psbA
2. matK
3. rbcL

**5. Give at least one example of a gene located in the SSC region.**
ndhF (NADH dehydrogenase subunit F).

**6. Give at least one example of a gene that occurs within an inverted repeat region. Is the gene shown more than once because of the duplicated IR regions?**
rrn16 (16S ribosomal RNA). Yes, it is shown more than once on the map because the inverted repeat regions are identical, duplicated segments of DNA situated on opposite sides of the genome.

**7. Choose one photosynthesis-related gene visible on your map. State its gene name and its general biological function.**
* Gene Name: psbA
* Biological Function: It encodes the D1 core reaction center protein of Photosystem II, which is essential for the light-dependent reactions of photosynthesis, specifically the splitting of water.

**8. What does the direction of the gene arrows or gene orientation tell you about transcription on the plastid genome?**
The arrows point in the direction of transcription (5' to 3'). Genes drawn on the outside of the circle are transcribed in one direction, while genes drawn on the inside of the circle are transcribed in the opposite direction, indicating which of the two DNA strands serves as the template for specific gene clusters.

**9. Look at the GC content graph. Does GC content appear uniform across the entire plastid genome, or does it vary among regions? Describe what you observe without over-interpreting it.**
The GC content is not uniform; it distinctly varies among regions. The inner graph shows that GC content is noticeably higher within the Inverted Repeat (IRA and IRB) regions compared to the single-copy (LSC and SSC) regions, appearing as peaks where ribosomal RNA genes are densely clustered.

**10. Why is a graphical plastid genome map useful compared with viewing only the raw DNA sequence or a long list of annotations?**
A graphical map provides an immediate, intuitive visual summary of the genome's architecture. It allows researchers to instantly observe the quadripartite structure, relative regional sizes, functional gene clustering, transcription directions, and structural inverted repeat blocks, which are difficult to visualize from raw text coordinates or long lists of base pair entries.
