1. **psaA (Photosystem I):** Encodes a core reaction center protein for Photosystem I, mediating light-driven electron transport.
2. **psbA (Photosystem II):** Encodes the D1 reaction center protein of Photosystem II, essential for splitting water during photosynthesis.
3. **atpA (ATP Synthase):** Encodes the alpha subunit of the chloroplast ATP synthase complex, which synthesizes ATP.

**9. Compare plastid and mitochondrial genomes. Give at least five similarities and five differences, considering location, biological role, inheritance, genome organization, gene content, copy number, and evolutionary behavior.**

* **Similarities:**
  i. Both are extranuclear organellar genomes possessing circular DNA molecules.
  ii. Both replicate independently of the host cell nuclear cycle.
  iii. Both share endosymbiotic evolutionary origins derived from bacterial ancestors (cyanobacteria and proteobacteria, respectively).
  iv. Both encode critical components essential for cellular energy metabolism and ATP synthesis.
  v. Both possess their own specialized internal transcription and translation machinery (ribosomal RNAs and tRNAs).

* **Differences:**
  i. **Primary Metabolic Role:** Plastids handle photosynthesis, carbon fixation, and lipid/amino acid synthesis; mitochondria handle cellular respiration and oxidative phosphorylation.
  ii. **Inheritance Pattern:** Chloroplasts are strictly maternally inherited in tomato and most angiosperms, whereas plant mitochondrial inheritance can occasionally vary.
  iii. **Genome Architecture:** Plastids exhibit a strict, conserved quadripartite structure (LSC-IR-SSC-IR); plant mitochondria frequently have fluid, multipartite, or subgenomic structures.
  iv. **Genome Size:** Plant chloroplast genomes are moderate and uniform (120–170 kb); plant mitochondria vary vastly in size, often expanding massively due to non-coding sequence accumulation.
  v. **Gene Content:** Chloroplasts retain a high count of photosynthetic and chlororespiratory genes; mitochondria focus on TCA cycle enzymes and oxidative phosphorylation chains.

**10. Explain the practical value of plastid genomes in research. List as many advantages as you can compared with the nuclear genome, including nuclear sex chromosomes where applicable, and also explain important limitations. Give one research question for which plastid data would be useful and one for which nuclear genomic data would be more appropriate.**

* **Advantages over Nuclear Genome:**
  * **High Production Volume:** Because there are thousands of copies of chloroplast DNA in a single plant cell, scientists can use them to produce very large amounts of a desired protein.
  * **Better Environmental Safety:** In most crops like tomato, chloroplasts are passed down only through the mother plant's eggs (not pollen), which greatly reduces the risk of genetically modified genes spreading to wild relatives via wind-blown pollen.
  * **Precise Gene Insertion:** New genes can be placed into exact, targeted spots via homologous recombination rather than landing randomly in the DNA, which prevents accidental damage to other important genes.
  * **Stable Expression:** The inserted genes are much less likely to be accidentally turned off or disrupted by neighboring DNA compared to genes in the nucleus.

* **Important Limitations:**
  * **Technical Difficulty:** It is technically challenging and often has low success rates when trying to genetically modify chloroplasts in certain crop species.
  * **Limited Protein Processing:** Chloroplasts cannot handle complex protein modifications (like adding specific sugar chains or glycosylation) as well as the main cell nucleus can.
  * **Pollen Restriction:** Because chloroplast DNA is only passed through the mother plant, you cannot use this method to pass traits through pollen if that is your breeding goal.

* **Research Question (Plastid Data Useful):** "Can chloroplast DNA sequences be used to trace the maternal ancestry and evolutionary divergence of wild *Solanum* species from modern cultivated tomatoes?"
* **Research Question (Nuclear Genomic Data):** "Which nuclear genes control the inheritance of fruit ripening speed and lycopene production in *Solanum lycopersicum*?"
